#myhandprint - Content Strategy System 2026

Small steps. Real lives.
Lasting change.

A complete content ideation system for Instagram - built around calm aesthetics, community belonging, and the radical idea that imperfect action is always better than perfect inaction.

01 Content Themes
The Living Kitchen
Food, waste, and nourishment as acts of care. Cooking from scraps, buying less, storing smarter. The kitchen as the most powerful sustainability space in any home.
Nature as Teacher
Short, grounding observations from the natural world - seasons, soil, water cycles - as mirrors for how humans can live more gently. Calm, meditative, awe-inspiring.
The Swap Series
One product, one habit, one small replacement - done beautifully and without guilt. The emphasis is on curiosity and experimentation, never obligation.
Handprint People
Real community stories. A student, a parent, a renter, a small business owner - each showing their imperfect, personal version of living a little more consciously.
Slow Rituals
Morning light through a window. Refilling a water bottle. Folding fabric bags. The quiet poetry of intentional daily moments that happen to also be sustainable.
The Budget Myth
Sustainability doesn't require a Goop lifestyle. This theme busts the premium price tag myth and shows affordable, accessible ways to act - for every income level.
Repair & Rewear
Mending, upcycling, second-hand finds, clothing swaps. Fashion-adjacent content that feels playful and creative - not austere or sacrificial.
City Nature
For urban dwellers - balcony gardens, windowsill herbs, local parks, community composting. Sustainability is not just for people with land.

03 15 Carousel Post Ideas
"7 things you can do right now, from your kitchen."

  • Store herbs in water like flowers
  • Freeze bread before it goes stale
  • Reuse pasta water for plants
  • Rinse plastic only if you must recycle it
  • Line your bin with newspaper, not bags

"The handprint vs footprint - what's the difference?"
  • Cover: "You've heard of your carbon footprint. Meet your handprint."

  • Handprint = what you actively improve
  • Both matter - neither makes you a villain or a hero
  • Examples of growing your handprint
  • Close: "What will you add to yours?"
Takeaway: Philosophy post - builds brand understanding and saves well.

04 10 Workshop Concepts
01 - The Slow Kitchen: Zero-Waste Cooking Together
Audience
Home cooks, families, curious beginners
Experience
Cook a meal using only scraps and seasonal produce. No recipe sheets - just intuition and conversation.
Take away: One scrap stock recipe, one composting habit, one new seasonal ingredient discovered.
02 - Mend & Mingle: Community Repair Café
Audience
All ages, especially those with clothing or objects they've been meaning to fix
Experience
Bring something broken. Leave with it repaired. Learn basic stitching, sewing, or gluing from community mentors.
Take away: The repaired item. The confidence to mend again. New friendships.
03 - Grow Something: Seed-to-Soil Starter
Audience
Urban dwellers, beginners, kids welcome
Experience
Plant seeds in a recycled container. Learn soil basics, watering rhythms, and which plants suit small spaces.
Take away: A live seedling in a recycled pot, labelled with their name.
04 - Natural Dye Lab: Colour from Kitchen Scraps
Audience
Creatives, makers, textile curious
Experience
Dye fabric swatches using onion skins, turmeric, beet - collected by participants beforehand. No chemical dyes.
Take away: Three dyed swatches, a personal colour recipe card, a creative connection to waste.
05 - Slow Sunday: A Morning of Mindful Rituals
Audience
Anyone feeling overwhelmed, burned out, or overscheduled
Experience
Two hours of slow living - morning tea ritual, journaling prompts, a short nature walk, no phones allowed.
Take away: A personalised slow morning ritual outline and a tin of loose-leaf tea to recreate it at home.
06 - My Handprint Map: Visualising Your Impact
Audience
Anyone wanting to understand their personal sustainability story
Experience
Create a personal "handprint map" - a visual reflection of current habits, future intentions, and what to let go of. Guided worksheets, facilitated conversation.
Take away: A personal handprint map. One concrete action for the next 30 days.
07 - Swap & Story: Clothing Exchange with Conversation
Audience
20s–40s, fashion-conscious but sustainability-curious
Experience
Bring 3 items, take 3 items. Each person shares the story of one thing they're giving away. Drinks provided, no receipts needed.
Take away: New clothes, someone else's story, a refreshed wardrobe at zero cost.
08 - For Kids: The Nature Detectives Walk
Audience
Children aged 5–12, with parents
Experience
A guided neighbourhood walk - observe, collect (only natural fallen items), draw, and ask questions. No lectures. Pure curiosity.
Take away: A hand-drawn "nature journal" page. One thing they noticed that they'll look for every week.
09 - The Home Audit: Finding What You Already Have
Audience
Families, renters, anyone starting out
Experience
Walk through your home virtually or live - identify what you already own that replaces single-use items. A facilitator helps you see resources, not gaps.
Take away: A room-by-room action list that costs nothing. A new lens for what "enough" looks like.
10 - Story Circle: Sharing Our Sustainability Journeys
Audience
Existing community members, repeat participants
Experience
An intimate seated circle - each person shares one moment of progress and one moment of failure from their sustainability journey. No advice-giving. Only listening.
Take away: Deep community belonging. The feeling of being understood, not judged.
05 Fun + Peace Content Formats
The 60-Second Still
One unedited, real-time scene. Rain. Dough rising. A candle. No voiceover, no text. Pure presence. Comments become a meditation space.
One Room, One Change
Weekly series - before/after (but soft and aesthetic, never staged). One space, one small shift, one sentence of story. Saves like crazy.
Seasonal Micro-Almanac
Each month, a single card: what's in season, what to forage, one weather pattern to notice. Posted on the 1st. A ritual people come back to.
The Handprint Portrait
One community member's hands - close up, no face required. Their one sustainable habit written in their own words. Monthly feature, UGC-driven.
The Quiet Prompt
A static image - beautiful, simple - with a one-line question in the caption. "What's one thing you repaired this month?" Generates genuine, intimate comments.
Growth Log
A recurring Stories series - tracking one plant, one habit, one community initiative - over weeks. Like a diary. Fosters investment and return.
The Monthly Reset Carousel
Posted on the last day of each month. Pages: what to let go of, what to begin, one nature observation for the coming month. Saved and revisited.
The This-or-That (Slow Edition)
Not fast-paced polls - thoughtful comparisons in Stories. "Buy once, use forever vs buy secondhand often." Community votes, results shared with nuance.
06 Awareness & Growth Strategy
01
The Community Handprint Wall
Invite followers to share a photo of their hands with a one-line sustainability habit. Repost weekly. Build a gallery of real people - not experts or influencers. The wall becomes the community.
02
Local Collaboration First
Partner with local farmers markets, neighbourhood refill shops, repair cafés, and small bakeries. Tag them genuinely. They share back. Hyper-local trust-building that national sustainability accounts can't replicate.
03
The "Pass It On" Monthly Challenge
One small action each month. Share it. Nominate one friend to try it too. Low pressure, snowball reach. Not a viral challenge - a quiet ripple.
04
Long-form Captions as Community Letters
Write captions that feel like letters to a friend - personal, honest, occasionally uncertain. People who read to the end become the most loyal followers. Depth beats breadth.
05
Guest Handprints
Invite non-influencer community members - a schoolteacher, a retired engineer, a 70-year-old who has composted for decades - to take over Stories for a day. Authenticity that paid collaborations cannot buy.
06
Cross-Pollinate with Aligned Niches
Follow, comment genuinely, and collaborate with slow living, foraging, minimalism, and local food accounts. Shared values - not shared audiences. The overlap creates natural discovery.
07
Save-Worthy Over Share-Worthy
Design every carousel for saving, not sharing. When the algorithm sees a save, it pushes reach. Practical guides, honest reflections, beautiful references - saved content lives forever.
08
IRL Workshops as Online Fuel
Every workshop generates content - participant hands, quiet moments, natural dye reveals. The offline experience becomes the most authentic online content you can post.
09
The "I tried it" Reply Loop
End every actionable post with: "If you try this, come back and tell me." Respond to every reply personally. The comment section becomes a conversation - and conversations are the algorithm's favourite signal.
10
Trust Signals Over Vanity Metrics
Resist follower-count obsession. A community of 3,000 people who act is more powerful - and more valuable to partners - than 30,000 passive scrollers. Grow slowly. Grow real.
07 Creative Direction & Visual Language
Primary Palette
Forest · Leaf · Sage · Mist · Linen
Accent Palette
Clay · Sand · Teal · Aqua · Charcoal
Typography Direction
Display: serif with warmth (Lora, Cormorant, Playfair). Body: clean humanist sans. Never geometric, never cold. Headline copy should feel handwritten in spirit - imperfect, human, warm.
Composition Rules
Generous white space. Subject never centred - offset to create breathing room. Texture over perfection. Objects shot at slight angles, never clinical.
Photography Direction
Always natural light. Never studio. Real homes, real kitchens, real soil. Imperfect backgrounds. Hands always visible. No models - real people living their real lives.
Recurring Motifs
Hands. Roots and stems. Glass jars. Worn cloth. Seeds. Soil texture. Water in motion. Objects with history. Nothing new, nothing shiny.
Visual Principles
Scale contrast - a single hand against a wide garden. A small seed on a vast wooden table. Human scale vs nature scale. Always in that tension.
White space as rest - let images breathe. Let text breathe. The page itself models slow living.
Concept over decoration - every visual should say something without text. The idea first. The beauty follows from it.
Texture as truth - soil under nails, worn fabric, cracked clay. Perfection signals performance. Texture signals real.
What to always avoid
Stock photography Bright white studio light Perfect symmetry Eco-aesthetic clichés Infographic overload Guilt-coded red tones Leaf logo everywhere Preaching captions
